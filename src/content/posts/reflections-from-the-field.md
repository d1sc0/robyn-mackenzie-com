Content design is rarely about making sentences sound pretty. Most of the time, it is about eliminating institutional fear.

When organisations communicate with the public, they often hide behind passive voice and legalistic jargon because it feels legally defensible. But to a citizen seeking support, bureaucratic language feels like a closed door.

### Before and After: Real Service Revisions

| Institutional Draft | User-Centred Revision | Why it works |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| *“Upon submission of requisite verification credentials, processing will commence.”* | **“Send us your ID, and we’ll check your application within 2 days.”** | Active voice, clear timeline, no jargon. |
| *“Failure to notify may result in discontinuation of service provision.”* | **“Let us know if your address changes so you don’t miss important letters.”** | Explains the consequence with care rather than punitive tone. |

> "Clear writing is a manifestation of institutional respect."

### My 3 Rules for Civic Content

1. **Write for the 10th percentile of reading ability, not the 90th.**
2. **Put the most urgent outcome in the first 5 words of the sentence.**
3. **Test with real people who do not work in the civil service.**
